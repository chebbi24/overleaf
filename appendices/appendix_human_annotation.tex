\chapter{Human Annotation Protocol}
\label{app:annotation}

This appendix records the annotation design used for the original v1 candidate set and the corresponding protocol to be applied to v2. The purpose is to preserve the distinction between absolute cultural appropriateness and relative Best-of-4 preference.

\section*{Annotator-facing task}
For each evaluation item, the annotator is shown one prompt followed by four complete candidate responses labelled A, B, C and D. Generator identity, Vericult outputs, reward-model scores, direct-judge decisions, evidence traces and provisional labels are hidden.

For each candidate, the annotator records a three-level cultural-appropriateness rating. The three levels correspond operationally to inappropriate, partially appropriate, and appropriate. After rating all four candidates, the annotator answers a forced comparative question asking which of A, B, C or D is the most culturally appropriate response.

The forced winner is a relative judgment. It must not be interpreted as evidence that the chosen response is absolutely appropriate. A prompt can contain four weak responses and still require one relative choice. Candidate-level ratings are therefore retained as the primary signal for single-response evaluation.

\section*{v1 realization}
The completed v1 annotation used five annotators over 30 prompts and four candidates per prompt. This yields 120 unique candidate responses, 600 candidate-level ratings and 150 Best-of-4 winner votes. The earlier thesis draft incorrectly described a \emph{No clear winner} choice and a 1--5 confidence field. Those fields were not part of the realized v1 study and are not reconstructed retrospectively.

\section*{v2 protocol}
The v2 survey uses the same two-part structure on the newly generated candidates. Candidate model identities remain blinded. If candidate order is randomized, the exact presented order is archived so that responses can be mapped back to canonical A--D identifiers. The survey export is frozen and hashed before the final automated-system comparison is computed.

\section*{Reference construction}
Candidate-level analysis uses the full distribution of 1/2/3 ratings, including median and mean summaries and an ordinal inter-rater agreement statistic. Relative Best-of-4 analysis uses the vote distribution over A--D. A unique majority or unique top-vote candidate can be used as the prompt-level human reference according to the pre-declared analysis rule; ties and highly split votes are retained as ambiguous rather than discarded silently.

The aggregate is referred to as a \emph{human reference judgment}, not cultural ground truth. Five student annotators cannot be assumed to represent every culture involved in the benchmark, and disagreement is itself informative for a culturally plural task.
