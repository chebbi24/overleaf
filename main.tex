\chapter*{Abstract}
Large language models are increasingly deployed across culturally diverse contexts, yet evaluating whether a generated response is culturally appropriate remains difficult. Cultural appropriateness is context-dependent, multidimensional and often involves social norms, pragmatic expectations, values, religious practices, institutional rules, identity and regional variation that cannot be reduced to factual correctness alone. Existing cultural benchmarks primarily evaluate cultural knowledge, value alignment, classification performance or relative response preferences, while reward models and direct LLM judges provide limited transparency into the evidential basis of their judgments.

This thesis introduces \textbf{Vericult}, a standalone evidence-grounded framework for verifying arbitrary prompt--response pairs. A literature-grounded operational framework defines ten cultural dimensions, D01--D10, spanning everyday practices, language and pragmatics, social etiquette, values, institutions, religion, family and gender, work and civic life, heritage, and identity and intergroup relations. Vericult extracts only prompt-supported context, first checks whether cultural reasoning is materially applicable, plans D01--D10 dimensions only when it is, rejects pure refusals or non-answers as non-assessable, deterministically grounds material response spans, generates neutral verification questions, acquires candidate-blind web evidence where retrieval is appropriate, freezes the resulting evidence, and then evaluates the candidate against the frozen evidence and rubric. Dimension judgments use scores 0, 1, 2 or \texttt{abstain}. The final outcome space distinguishes \texttt{not\_culturally\_applicable}, \texttt{not\_assessable}, \texttt{insufficient\_evidence}, and the three substantive cultural labels rather than collapsing these cases into one uncertainty state.

A separate pre-freeze positive-control experiment evaluated 120 source-endorsed prompt--response pairs drawn evenly from six public cultural-alignment datasets using the same applicability/assessability result semantics later restored into the definitive verifier. After reruns, 116 of 120 cases completed; among the 102 cases receiving a substantive numeric cultural assessment, none was labelled culturally inappropriate. Because the source responses were positively selected and heterogeneous, this result is interpreted as coarse source consistency and cross-dataset feasibility rather than an accuracy estimate or calibrated human-preference prediction.
