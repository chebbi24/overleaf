\chapter*{Abstract}
Large language models are increasingly deployed across culturally diverse contexts, yet evaluating whether a generated response is culturally appropriate remains difficult. Cultural appropriateness is context-dependent, multidimensional and often involves social norms, pragmatic expectations, values, religious practices, institutional rules, identity and regional variation that cannot be reduced to factual correctness alone. Existing cultural benchmarks primarily evaluate cultural knowledge, value alignment, classification performance or relative response preferences, while reward models and direct LLM judges provide limited transparency into the evidential basis of their judgments.

This thesis introduces \textbf{Vericult}, a standalone evidence-grounded framework for verifying arbitrary prompt--response pairs. A literature-grounded operational framework defines ten dimensions of cultural appropriateness covering areas such as everyday practices, language, social norms, values, religion, family, institutions, heritage, and identity. Building on this framework, Vericult assesses whether an LLM response is culturally appropriate by grounding its judgments in external evidence and by explicitly handling cases in which cultural evaluation is not applicable, not assessable, or insufficiently supported. The framework is designed to provide more transparent and interpretable judgments than opaque preference-based evaluation approaches.

Vericult is evaluated across multiple cultural-alignment benchmarks and against established model-based evaluation approaches. The experiments examine whether evidence-grounded verification can provide reliable and interpretable assessments of cultural appropriateness across diverse cultural contexts. The results indicate that the proposed framework is capable of distinguishing culturally appropriate from problematic responses while providing a more transparent basis for its judgments than conventional preference-based evaluators. Overall, the findings support evidence-grounded verification as a promising approach for the systematic evaluation of cultural appropriateness in large language model outputs.

Overall, this work shows that cultural appropriateness can be evaluated as an explicit verification problem rather than solely as an opaque model preference, providing a more interpretable basis for analysing culturally sensitive language model behaviour.
